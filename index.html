Overview

Wave–current interactions can substantially modify coastal water levels during tropical cyclones. Yet how their multiple, bidirectional processes collectively shape non-tidal extremes remains poorly understood. We investigate this question in the South China Sea using COAWST simulations and controlled process-isolation experiments. We find that large non-tidal water-level responses preferentially occur in strongly coupled states dominated by wave effects on currents.

COAWST-SCS organizes a South China Sea application of the Coupled Ocean–Atmosphere–Wave–Sediment Transport (COAWST) modeling system. It connects local case configuration with wave–current process diagnostics, the WCIM/WCID framework, and manuscript analysis code.

Research Workflow

01

Define coupled states

Relate current-speed and wave-height responses to WCI and non-tidal water levels.

02

Resolve dynamical processes

Distinguish native diagnostics, equivalent scales, configuration contrasts and residual proxies.

03

Identify environmental structure

Separate process activity, reconstruction importance and response associations.

04

Compare spatial settings

Examine terrain contrasts, high-response locations and distant coastal analogues.

Why the Name?

COAWST-SCS is built on the COAWST modeling framework to investigate wave–current coupling and non-tidal water-level responses in the South China Sea. COAWST is developed by USGS and its contributors; SCS denotes our South China Sea case implementation and research workflow.

Key Features

ROMS–SWAN case configuration for a South China Sea application.

Process-level activity and response diagnostics, with explicit distinctions between native terms, configuration contrasts and residual proxies.

WCI_M and WCI_D for complementary descriptions of coupling magnitude and directional dominance.
